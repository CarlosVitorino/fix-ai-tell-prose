# How to make writing sound human

Human writing is not a list of approved words. It has a purpose, a point of view and
details that belong to its subject. Edit for those qualities rather than disguising
how the draft was produced.

## Meaning first

- Say what happened, what the product does or what the reader can do. Prefer a clear
  noun and verb to a claim about importance, excellence or potential.
- Keep useful specifics. Names, mechanisms, examples and limits give a reader something
  to understand and check. Never invent them to make a sentence sound more convincing.
- Test generic praise by imagining it on another company's page. If it fits unchanged,
  look for the actual fact underneath it. Ask for missing evidence when needed.
- Make references clear. A reader should know what "it", "one", "the winners" or
  "what survives" refers to without decoding the metaphor.
- Use metaphors when they clarify. Do not make readers learn several metaphors for the
  same feature. Keep the useful name for a thing instead of cycling synonyms.
- Distinguish a claim from its evidence. Blanket statements about competitors, customers
  or outcomes need support even when they contain no stock AI vocabulary.

## Voice and rhythm

- Preserve the writer's opinions, humour, uncertainty and characteristic wording when
  they serve the reader. Do not add a personality the owner never supplied.
- Vary sentence length naturally. A clear long sentence can be better than a sequence
  of fragments. Short is not automatically human.
- Notice repetition across the whole piece: identical openings, three-part lists,
  negative contrasts, dramatic reveals and little slogans after every section. Keep a
  structure that explains something; change it when it becomes a mannerism.
- A contrast should clarify a real distinction. A list should contain useful items.
  Neither earns an automatic exemption merely because its nouns are concrete.
- Keep the real point without the performance around it. Introductions that announce
  the topic, commentary that tells readers a fact is important, and clever final lines
  often delay or repeat information.
- End on a useful fact or next action. A button beside a slogan does not make the slogan
  informative; judge the words themselves.

## Make the smallest effective edit

Use ordinary words when they express the meaning accurately. Remove filler and redundant
hedges, but retain uncertainty that belongs to the claim. Keep established technical terms
when the audience needs them. Formatting and punctuation should make reading easier, not
impose a uniform style.

For example, "The app serves as a central hub for your notes" can become "The app keeps
your notes in one place" if that preserves its actual function. "Our service transforms
your workflow" needs an owner explanation of what changes, not an invented time saving.

Read the result aloud in your head. Would this owner say it? Is the meaning easier to
follow? Does the page still do its job? Leave a strong passage alone and explain a proposed
change in terms the owner can disagree with.
